Low-voltage metal-enclosed switchgear

LX-4000 Draw-Out Switchgear, 480Y/277 VAC

This document defines the ratings, construction, testing, and submittal requirements for LX-4000 switchgear lineups. Engineers use it for review and approval. Contractors use it for ordering and installation.

Writing sample. LX-4000 is a fictional product. Ratings and clauses show typical industry practice and are not for construction.

1.0Scope

  1. 1.1This specification covers indoor, low-voltage, metal-enclosed switchgear with draw-out power circuit breakers for service entrance and distribution at 600 VAC and below.
  2. 1.2Each lineup includes the main breaker section, feeder breaker sections, metering and controls, and the bus and enclosure that connect them.
  3. 1.3Transformers, cable, conduit, and field-installed equipment outside the switchgear enclosure are not in scope.

2.0Applicable standards

The switchgear is designed, built, and tested to the latest editions of these standards. Where they conflict, the stricter requirement applies.

  • UL 1558
  • IEEE C37.20.1
  • IEEE C37.13
  • UL 1066
  • ANSI C37.16
  • ANSI C37.51
  • NFPA 70 (NEC)
  • NEMA SG 5

4.0Construction

  1. 4.1Each vertical section has separate compartments for the breaker, the main bus, and outgoing cable. Grounded steel barriers separate the compartments.
  2. 4.2Breakers rack between Connected, Test, and Disconnected positions with the compartment door closed. A position indicator is visible from the front.
  3. 4.3Mechanical interlocks prevent racking a closed breaker and prevent closing a breaker between positions.
  4. 4.4Automatic shutters cover the primary stabs when a breaker is withdrawn. Shutters can be padlocked closed.
  5. 4.5A copper ground bus runs the full length of the lineup and bonds to every section.
  6. 4.6Each lineup provides a connection point for a remote racking device so operators can rack breakers from outside the arc flash boundary.

5.0Power circuit breakers

  1. 5.1Breakers are draw-out, stored-energy, low-voltage power circuit breakers that meet IEEE C37.13 and UL 1066.
  2. 5.2Each breaker has an electronic trip unit with long-time, short-time, instantaneous, and ground-fault (LSIG) functions.
  3. 5.3Trip units have a maintenance mode that lowers the instantaneous pickup to reduce incident energy during work. The mode shows a local indicator when active.

6.0Factory testing

Each lineup passes production tests per IEEE C37.20.1 before shipment. Certified test reports are included with the final documentation.

7.0Submittal package contents

Submit the following for engineering review. Approval drawings must match the order before release to manufacturing.

  • Front elevations with dimensions and weights
  • Floor plan and conduit entry areas
  • One-line diagram
  • Three-line diagram
  • Bill of material
  • Breaker and trip unit data sheets
  • Time-current curves
  • Control wiring diagrams
  • Nameplate schedule
  • Short-circuit and bus ratings summary

8.0Installation and field testing

  1. 8.1Install on a level concrete pad and anchor per the seismic and anchoring details on the approved drawings.
  2. 8.2Torque bus splice and cable lug connections to the values on the installation instructions. Record each torque value on the installation checklist.
  3. 8.3Perform acceptance testing per ANSI/NETA ATS before energizing. Testing includes insulation resistance, breaker trip testing, and verification of protective settings against the coordination study.
  4. 8.4Apply arc flash labels from the site's incident energy analysis before the equipment is placed in service.
